A knife that is dropped on a concrete floor is a knife with a chipped edge you cannot see until the line trips. Storage and handling SOPs cost almost nothing and prevent the most expensive failure modes — micro-chips, rust, geometry drift — before they reach the line.

> **One-line summary:** *Store in foam-lined cases, edge-up, in a dry room. Apply rust-preventive oil for storage longer than 30 days. Use a dedicated knife cart, not a forklift. Inspect every knife on receipt and on every re-grind return.*

---

## The four storage rules

1. **Foam-lined case, edge-up.** Slot dividers between knives. No contact between knife edges.
2. **Dry room.** 30–50 % relative humidity. Air-conditioned in humid climates.
3. **Rust-preventive oil.** For storage longer than 30 days, apply a thin film of light mineral oil. Remove before mounting.
4. **Inventory tracking.** Each knife has a tracking sheet with re-grind count, dates, and operator initials.

A knife stored in a humid drawer with other knives develops three problems simultaneously: surface rust, micro-chips from edge contact, and geometry drift from sitting in a deformed position. All three show up in the first cut after the knife is mounted.

---

## The handling rules

- **Two hands, both on the body.** Never carry a knife by the edge, by the bore, or by a single hand on the OD.
- **Dedicated knife cart.** A foam-lined cart with handle and locking wheels. Not a forklift. Not a cardboard box.
- **Foam or rubber mat on the workbench.** When the knife is set down, it sits on the mat, not on a steel table.
- **Visual inspection on receipt.** Look for chips, cracks, rust, and decarburised surface before mounting.
- **No loose knives on the line.** A knife in a pocket is a chipped knife. Use the cart, not the pocket.

---

## Common failures

1. **Knife chips on receipt.** A new knife was dropped during shipping. Inspect immediately on receipt, before mounting.
2. **Rust on a 30-day-old knife.** Stored without oil in a humid environment. Apply oil before storage; re-oil every 90 days.
3. **Micro-chips on the side of the disc.** Two knives stored touching. Use slot dividers.
4. **Geometry drift on a thin slitter.** A 1.5 mm slitter stored on a flat surface without support warps under its own weight. Use a curved foam insert.
5. **Knife "lost" in the tool crib.** Inventory tracking is missing. The 5 minutes per shift to log a knife return saves the 1-hour search when a knife is needed urgently.

## The inspection checklist on receipt

For every new knife:

- [ ] OD, ID, thickness per drawing (± 0.02 mm)
- [ ] Hardness 5-point file test (within ± 1 HRC of target)
- [ ] Edge hone under 10× loupe (within ± 2 µm of spec)
- [ ] Surface finish Ra (within spec)
- [ ] Visual: chips, cracks, rust, decarb
- [ ] Mill certificate with ladle chemistry (and cobalt content for carbide)
- [ ] Signed and dated on the tracking sheet

A knife that fails any of these is rejected, not mounted. The 5 minutes of inspection saves the hours of line troubleshooting if the knife is sub-spec.

## Storage room setup

A proper industrial knife storage room has:

- 30–50 % relative humidity control (dehumidifier in humid climates)
- Foam-lined case racks (vertical, with slot dividers)
- A dedicated inspection bench with 10× loupe, surface-roughness tester, hardness files, micrometer, and dial indicator
- A separate workbench for re-grind return and inspection
- A clearly labelled "incoming / in-service / retired" workflow

The setup cost is €5,000–€15,000 for a typical shop. The cost of one chip event (€1,000–€10,000 in scrap and downtime) is recovered inside the first year.

## Field case

**Case: paper converter, 8 slitting lines.** The customer was getting 14 days from a D2 slitter, with 30 % of knives showing micro-chips on receipt from the re-grind shop. The tool crib had a single shared drawer for all knives. We specified foam-lined case racks with slot dividers, a 5-point inspection on receipt, and an inventory log. Micro-chips on receipt dropped to 2 %. Service life went from 14 to 19 days. Annual savings: €22,000.

## The SOP

For industrial knife storage and handling:

1. Store in foam-lined cases, edge-up, with slot dividers.
2. Maintain 30–50 % RH in the storage room.
3. Apply rust-preventive oil for storage longer than 30 days.
4. Inspect every knife on receipt and on every re-grind return.
5. Use a dedicated knife cart, not a forklift.
6. Track every knife (re-grind count, dates, operator).
7. Retire any knife with chips, decarburisation, or below-spec geometry.
