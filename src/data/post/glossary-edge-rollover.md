**Edge Rollover** is the deformation of a cutting edge where the substrate pushes the steel over instead of cutting it cleanly. The rolled edge looks like a wire edge that has folded over, usually visible as a bright lip on the back face of the knife.

## Common causes

- Hardness too low for the substrate (HRC 56 cutting 304 stainless)
- Edge operating above its tempering temperature (D2 above 450 °C)
- Impact load on a soft knife
- Repeated thermal cycling (thermal fatigue plus rollover)

## Fixes

- Increase hardness by 2–4 HRC points
- Move to a hot-hardness steel (M2 HSS)
- Add a hot-hardness coating (TiAlN, AlCrN)
- Add active cooling to drop edge temperature below tempering point
