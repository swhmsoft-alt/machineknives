**undefined** is the nominal abrasive particle size of a grinding wheel, measured by the number of openings per linear inch in a sieve that the particles just pass through. A 60-grit wheel has coarser particles than a 120-grit wheel.

## Typical grit sizes for blade grinding

- Roughing: 36–60 grit (aluminium-oxide, for shear blades and thick slitters)
- General re-grind: 60–80 grit (CBN or aluminium-oxide)
- Finishing: 100–120 grit (CBN)
- Hone restoration: 200–400 grit (felt wheel with diamond paste)

## Coarser vs finer

- Coarser grit: faster stock removal, rougher surface, more heat input
- Finer grit: slower stock removal, finer surface, less heat input
- Too fine on a high-stock job: the wheel loads and burns the workpiece
- Too coarse on a precision finish: leaves grind marks and requires extra passes
